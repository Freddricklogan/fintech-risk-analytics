# Case Study — FinTech Risk Analytics

**Repository:** [fintech-risk-analytics](https://github.com/Freddricklogan/fintech-risk-analytics) · **Live demo:** [freddricklogan.github.io/fintech-risk-analytics](https://freddricklogan.github.io/fintech-risk-analytics/) · **Author:** Freddrick Logan

---

## 1. Who has this problem

Anyone who has to read a risk dashboard they did not build: an endowment or pension committee, a treasury team at a public institution, a student handed "the platform" in a financial-analytics course, and the reviewer — I have been one, in analytics consulting — asked whether the numbers can be defended. The dashboard can produce a Value-at-Risk figure. It usually cannot say which method, on which weights, from which data, or whether the same button gives the same figure tomorrow.

## 2. The problem, as a scenario

A committee sees "VaR (95%) 2.1%" on a slide. Which VaR? Parametric — historical is also shown, differs, and nobody says why. Weighted how? By a column someone typed, not by the holdings on the previous slide, which value the same names differently. From what history? A file with a third of its dates on weekends and returns that do not match their closes. Reproducible? No; the simulation was unseeded. The previous version of this tool was that slide. I wrote it, and its audit is in the repository.

## 3. What it costs to leave it alone

A limit set on a figure that changes with the seed; a hedge sized to a portfolio that is not the one held; a class taught to trust a number because it has a percent sign. I will not attach a figure — the capital behind a VaR line varies by orders of magnitude and this tool holds no real positions. What is certain is that every defect in the audit is visible to a careful reader in an afternoon, which a committee does not have.

## 4. The approach, and the alternative I rejected

I rebuilt the tool so that method, weights and data are all on the page. Holdings are valued by market price and those weights are used everywhere. Three VaR figures sit side by side — historical with no distributional assumption, parametric from the same moments, Monte Carlo through a Cholesky factor of the empirical covariance — each with its dollar amount and its assumption stated, with expected shortfall, Sharpe and drawdown beside them. The projection is a seeded geometric Brownian motion with percentile bands and the closed-form expectation beside the simulated mean; Black–Scholes prices carry five Greeks tested against finite differences. The 252-day history is synthetic, generated by a seeded script in the repository, labelled as such, and tested for business days and return–close consistency. A holdings CSV imports in the browser; the parser lists rejected rows and uncovered symbols.

The alternative I rejected was a live market-data feed. It would make the demo look like a product and every number unreproducible between visits. A synthetic history whose construction is in the repository teaches the methods and can be checked.

## 5. What the code does today

Real: a tested RFC-4180 CSV parser with validation; valuation and sector allocation; historical, parametric and Monte Carlo VaR, expected shortfall, Sharpe, drawdown, beta, covariance and correlation; a seeded GBM projection with percentile bands; Black–Scholes with Greeks and a payoff diagram; Mulberry32, Box–Muller, Acklam's inverse normal and Hart's Φ; Chart.js pinned with SRI plus a vendored fallback; the Executive Shell with KPIs from live state; CSV import that never leaves the browser.

Simulated: the market. The history is a seeded correlated GBM with market and sector factors; CSV prices are illustrative; nothing is a quote or a forecast, and the page says so.

Worth knowing: Monte Carlo VaR draws jointly normal returns, so it agrees with parametric VaR by construction — the honest comparison is with historical, which sits higher here; the synthetic year was a down year for the market factor, so the Sharpe ratio is negative and the page reports it.

## 6. Evidence

Measured locally with the commands CI runs: 22 tests passing across five files; 99.55% statement and 93.12% branch coverage of the pure modules; ESLint and html-validate clean. Tests fix historical VaR and expected shortfall by hand on a ten-value series, parametric VaR against its formula, Black–Scholes at 4.6150 and 3.3728 with put–call parity to 1e-9, five Greeks against central finite differences, Cholesky reconstruction and rejection, Monte Carlo VaR within 3% of parametric, and the dataset's 252 business days with returns matching closes to 1e-5. Headless Chrome: zero console errors; portfolio $243,412, P&L $47,977 (24.55%); VaR 95% historical 2.34%, parametric 2.08%, Monte Carlo 2.08%; Sharpe −0.38, maximum drawdown 20.41%; projection mean $269,450 against closed-form $269,012; four tour steps; no horizontal scroll at 1280 or 400 pixels.

## 7. What it would take to run this in production

As a teaching console and a review checklist it is production now. As a risk system it would need a market-data source with an audit trail, corporate-action handling, a shrinkage covariance estimator, a fat-tailed or bootstrap Monte Carlo, positions and limits from the book of record, and a results log so every figure is reproducible with its inputs. Weeks of data engineering with a model-validation function beside it; the three-method comparison carries over.

## 8. Limits and next steps

Equities only, one-day horizon, jointly normal Monte Carlo, a synthetic history independent of the CSV's prices, no VaR backtest. Next: a Kupiec exception test, a Student-t Monte Carlo so the third figure diverges from the second, a horizon scaler with the square-root-of-time caveat stated, and component VaR per holding.

## 9. Who should look at this

**Hiring manager:** evidence that I build analytics whose method, data and reproducibility are visible, and audit my own earlier work in writing.
**Consulting client:** the questions I put to any risk dashboard — which method, weights, data, seed — answered on one page.
**Engineer:** read `src/risk.js` and `tests/risk.test.js` for the three estimators and their hand-checked cases, and `scripts/generate-market-data.mjs` for the seeded history.
